## I. THE CONVERGENCE

Two independent bodies of work converge on the same conclusion from opposite directions.

**From the math:** Multiplicative composition (E = ∏ xᵢ^αᵢ) proves axiomatically that marginal returns are highest where the dimension is lowest. Zero in any dimension collapses the product regardless of how large other dimensions are. Investment at the bottom is optimization, not charity. The proof is at duke-of-beans/multiplicative-composition; the research page is at davidkirsch.me/research.

**From the documentation:** The Crisis Capitalism corpus (130+ parts, ~2,300 pages, 43+ extraction mechanisms, $1.7–3.4T annual extraction) documents 150 years of a system doing the structural opposite — investing at the top where marginal returns are lowest, extracting from the bottom where marginal returns are highest. The FPP synthesis (7 phases, avg corroboration weight 4.6/5.0) maps the capture architecture, the distraction machine that obscures it, and the dollar cost to the median household ($12,500–$30,600/year in documented extraction).

The math says this is provably suboptimal. The documentation says it is deliberate.

The essay does not need both bodies of work to make its argument. The math alone is sufficient — it closes the question of where returns are highest. But the FPP corpus answers the question the math can't: *why does the suboptimal allocation persist?* It persists because it is profitable for the people making the allocation.

### Section 2: Lens 1 — The Individual (Housing First)

**Core claim:** Piecemeal social programs fail because they assume additive composition. Housing First works because it addresses the zero.

**Evidence (all W4-W5):**
- CDC/CPSTF systematic review of 26 studies: Housing First decreases homelessness, increases stability, improves quality of life. Every $1 invested returns $1.44 in cost savings. [W5 — CDC, NLIHC]
- Canadian At Home/Chez Soi RCT (N=950, 5 cities, 2009-2011): Housing First with ACT achieved 73% stable housing at one year vs 31% for treatment-as-usual. 69% of intervention costs offset by savings elsewhere. [W5 — Psychiatry Online, peer-reviewed]
- VA demonstration: Housing First reduced time to placement from 223 to 35 days; retention 98% vs 86%. [W4 — PMC/VA]
- Finland national program (2008-2022): long-term homelessness reduced 68%. Shelter beds in Helsinki dropped from ~600 to near zero. €270M invested. Finland is the only EU country where homelessness declined. [W5 — Finnish government, Pathfinders, Housing First Europe Hub]
- NLIHC systematic review (20 studies): benefits of Housing First outweigh costs. [W4]

**The multiplicative reading:** Housing First works because housing is the zero. When housing = 0, job training × healthcare × education × support = 0. Address the zero, and every other investment starts compounding. The additive model predicts that adding job training helps regardless of housing status. It doesn't. The multiplicative model predicts it doesn't. It's right.

**The bootstrap critique:** "Pull yourself up by your bootstraps" is mathematically incoherent when any dimension is at zero. Effort in nonzero dimensions multiplies by zero. The math doesn't have a bootstrap exception.

### Section 5: The Inversion — Trickle-Down as Provably Suboptimal

**Core claim:** Trickle-down economics invests where the partial derivative is shallowest. The empirical record confirms: investment at the top produces no measurable growth, only higher concentration. Investment at the bottom produces outsized, measurable returns.

**Evidence — Top-end investment produces nothing (W4-W5):**
- Hope & Limberg (2020/2022, Socio-Economic Review 20(2):539-559): 30 major tax cuts for the rich across 18 OECD countries, 1965-2015. Result: increased top-1% income share, NO statistically significant effect on real GDP per capita or unemployment. [W5 — peer-reviewed]
- Hungerford (2012, CRS R42729): Top rate 90%+ in 1950s → GDP averaged 4.2%. Top rate 35% in 2000s → GDP averaged 1.7%. No evidence 65 years of top-rate reductions are associated with saving, investment, or productivity. Associated with rising concentration: top-0.1% share from 4.2% (1945) to 12.3% (2007). Report withdrawn under Senate Republican pressure, reissued unchanged. [W5 — CRS]
- Ostry, Berg & Tsangarides (2014, IMF SDN 14/02): Lower net inequality robustly correlated with faster, more durable growth. Redistribution generally pro- or growth-neutral. The equity-efficiency tradeoff is "largely illusory." [W5 — IMF]
- Kansas (2012-2017): Brownback's "real live experiment." Top rate cut ~30%, pass-through taxed at zero. Result: GDP grew 3.8% vs 7% nationally (2013-2016). Employment +2.6% vs +6.5% nationally. Bond rating cut. Republican legislature repealed cuts over governor's veto (June 6, 2017). Brookings' William Gale: "one of the cleanest experiments... it showed that they were a failure." [W4 — CBPP, Brookings, TPC, NPR, CAP]

**Evidence — Bottom-end investment produces outsized returns (W5):**
- GiveDirectly/Kenya (Egger et al., Econometrica 90(6):2603-2643, Nov 2022): $1,000 transfers to 10,500+ poor households across 653 villages. Fiscal multiplier of 2.5-2.7x. Large positive spillovers to non-recipients. Minimal price inflation. [W5 — peer-reviewed, NBER WP 26600]
- BRAC Graduation (Banerjee, Duflo et al., Science 348(6236):1260799, 2015): Multidimensional "big push" (asset + consumption + training + coaching + savings + health) across 6 countries, ~11,000 households. Returns 133% (Ghana) to 433% (India). Multidimensional package outperformed single-dimension interventions within the same RCT. [W5 — peer-reviewed, J-PAL]
- Moving to Opportunity (Chetty, Hendren & Katz, AER 106(4):855-902, 2016): Children who moved to lower-poverty neighborhoods before age 13 earned 31% more in adult income ($3,477 above $11,270 control mean). Returns largest at lowest baseline, youngest ages. [W5 — peer-reviewed, NBER WP 21156]

**The multiplicative reading:** The juxtaposition IS the proof. Top-end investment → zero measurable growth (Hope-Limberg, Hungerford, Kansas). Bottom-end investment → 2.5-4.3x returns (Kenya, BRAC, MTO). Trickle-down invests where ∂E/∂xᵢ ≈ 0. Social investment invests where ∂E/∂xᵢ is maximal. One is provably suboptimal. The other is provably optimal. The math closed the question.

**The BRAC graduation study is the keystone.** Within a single RCT, the multidimensional "big push" (raising several low dimensions simultaneously — the multiplicative approach) beat single-component interventions (raising one dimension — the additive approach). Additive vs multiplicative tested directly. Multiplicative won.

### Section 7: Historical Parallels and the Marshall Plan Inversion

**Marshall Plan (1948-1952):** $13.3B (~$150B 2024 dollars, ~5% of 1948 US GDP), ~90% in grants. Participating countries' GNP rose ~33%, industrial production +35%. The multiplicative reading: Europe's human capital and institutional trust survived the war (nonzero); the binding constraint was destroyed infrastructure (near-zero). Fixing the zero dimension let the product recover. Caveat: ERP aid averaged only ~2.5% of recipients' GNP; historians dispute raw dollar causation. The claim is mechanism (binding constraint relief), not volume.

**Nordic model:** Early universal investment in lowest dimensions (education, healthcare, housing, childcare). Result: Great Gatsby Curve — highest mobility, lowest inequality. Suggestive, not standalone proof (Landersø-Heckman IGE measurement critique applies).

**Structural adjustment programs (1980s-90s):** IMF/World Bank forced cuts to health, education, welfare across Sub-Saharan Africa/Latin America. Thomson, Kentikelenis & Stubbs (2017): detrimental association with child/maternal health. SAPs associated with 70-90+ excess deaths per 100,000 (respiratory/TB). Growth in least-developed countries fell below prior decades. Cutting the lowest dimensions produced contraction — the multiplicative downside, the mirror of the Marshall Plan.

**UK austerity (2010-2019):** Linked to ~190,000 excess deaths (Berman & Hovland, LSE/KCL 2024; contested — Full Fact, Prof. Martin Roland). Food banks from ~35 centres to ~1,300. Debt rose from 65% to 98% GDP. Austerity failed on its own terms.

**The pattern:** Marshall Plan (fix the zero → recovery). Nordic model (invest in the floor → compounding returns). SAPs (cut the floor → collapse). ## VI. CAVEATS AND FALSIFICATION CRITERIA

**The thesis would weaken if:**
- A high-quality RCT showed single-dimension interventions matching the BRAC multidimensional package (contradicting the multiplicative advantage).
- A credible study found statistically significant, replicable growth effect from top-end tax cuts after the Hope-Limberg controls (supporting trickle-down).
- A failed state lost institutional quality to the bottom percentile WITHOUT disproportionate output collapse (contradicting the cliff prediction).
- OBBBA's Medicaid work requirements, when implemented (Jan 1, 2027), produce Arkansas's ~25% coverage loss → cliff thesis confirmed. If state implementation is generous and coverage loss stays well below CBO's 5.3M → linear/optimistic case strengthens.

**None of these currently exists in the literature.**

**Caveats to flag in the essay:**
- Marshall Plan causation is genuinely contested; claim mechanism, not dollar volume.
- Great Gatsby Curve / IGE has known measurement critique (Landersø-Heckman, Mankiw).
- UK austerity excess-death estimates (190K-335K) are peer-reviewed but causally contested (Full Fact, Prof. Roland).
- ARRA vs UK is a natural experiment, not controlled (monetary regime, currency sovereignty, eurozone exposure differ).
- WGI's own methodology cautions most year-to-year changes are not statistically significant.
- CBO estimates are model-dependent; present ranges, not point estimates.
- FY2026 HUD/education cuts are budget REQUESTS, not enacted. OBBBA is enacted law.
